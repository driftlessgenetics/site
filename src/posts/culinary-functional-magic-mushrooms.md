People use "mushroom" for very different things. The categories are defined by why you eat the mushroom, not by biology, and some species fit more than one.

## Culinary

Culinary mushrooms are grown for taste and texture: button, shiitake, oyster, chanterelle, morel. You cook them and eat them as food.

Lion's mane is one of the few functional species that is also a good culinary one. Seared, its shreddy flesh is often compared to crab or scallop.

## Functional

Functional mushrooms are used for possible benefits beyond nutrition. The usual examples are lion's mane, reishi, chaga, cordyceps, and turkey tail.

- **Lion's mane** contains hericenones and erinacines, which researchers study for effects on nerve growth.
- **Reishi** is tough and bitter, so it is taken as a tea or extract. Its triterpenes and beta-glucans are the compounds of interest.
- **Chaga** is a dense, woody mass that grows mostly on birch. It is also taken as a tea or extract.

The evidence is early. Most human studies are small, and none of these are medicines. Talk to a doctor before using them if you take medication or have a medical condition.

## Magic

Magic mushrooms are *Psilocybe* and related species that contain psilocybin and psilocin, which cause psychedelic effects. They are not food and not a functional supplement.

Psilocybin is a Schedule I substance under US federal law. A few states have decriminalized or regulated it, and rules differ widely, so check your local law. Driftless Genetics does not sell or advise on psilocybin mushrooms.

## Where the lines blur

| | Eaten for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Culinary | Flavor and texture | Shiitake, oyster, morel |
| Functional | Possible health effects | Reishi, chaga, lion's mane |
| Magic | Psychoactive effects | *Psilocybe* species |

Lion's mane sits in both the culinary and functional rows. Reishi and chaga sit only in the functional one.

## What liquid culture is

Liquid culture is living mycelium suspended in nutrient solution. You use it to inoculate grain or substrate at home and grow your own mushrooms. Our cultures are lion's mane, reishi, and chaga.
